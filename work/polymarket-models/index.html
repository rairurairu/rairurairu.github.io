From forecasting to market making

The research began with football match forecasting. Built on more than 40,000 historical matches, the models beat the standard Elo rating on 3,504 matches they had never seen. I then moved the research to market making on Polymarket, where the question changes from “who will win?” to “at what price is it safe to quote?”.

What I decided

  • Size the risk before quotingBefore quoting, I had 777,588 historical orders replayed. Prices hit sell quotes about 4.5 times as often as buy quotes: the main risk a market maker has to price in.
  • Retire what the data says can't workAcross 1,444 simulated decisions, a forecasting signal showed no edge, however strong its predicted advantage. I stopped tuning it and redirected the research, before any money was at risk.
  • Withdraw a result I couldn't verifyA promising-looking replay was withdrawn when the market conditions behind it could not be confirmed from the data.
  • Keep a person in charge of real-world actionsAgents build and test the trading code, but only a person can start anything that trades, with size and loss limits.

What it shows

Agent-driven research is only as good as the judgement deciding what to stop.

AI agents make research fast. The value I add is the discipline around them: measure the risk first, test only on data the model hasn't seen, and withdraw anything that cannot be verified.
