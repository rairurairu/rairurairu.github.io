LLM Brand Recommendation Benchmark

AI-native market research. When a customer asks an AI assistant “what are the best brands for this?”, which brands does it recommend, and are they the right ones? I turned that manual research question into an AI-run benchmark.

The problem

More customers now ask AI assistants which brands to buy, so what those assistants recommend matters to every brand. Marketers call it AI visibility. Measuring it needs two things that are hard to get right: a verified map of which brands belong to which product category, and a fair way to test the assistant's answers against it.

What I decided

  • Redesign when the first approach stallsMy first approach, which started from well-known brands and merged several product catalogues, validated one category. I redesigned the method around a single product taxonomy, with categories taken from the taxonomy rather than from famous brands. Within two days, 50 of 51 categories validated against verified brand lists.
  • Fix the ground truth, not the modelMost failures came from incomplete reference brand lists, not from AI error. Completing the lists turned 13 of 14 failures into passes.
  • Treat answer variance as part of the testThe same question can score differently from one conversation to the next. Each test runs on a fixed version of the data in a fresh conversation, and every answer is stored with the reason it was matched.
  • Cut cost without fooling myselfI cut evaluation cost by two thirds, and AI answers count only as leads until they are independently verified. I report the first-attempt pass rate (37 of 51) as the clean measure.
  • Turn messy answers into clean dataProduct lines map to their brands, aliases and parent brands are resolved, and nothing is counted twice.

The result

A database of 943 verified category–brand links across 480 brands, and a reusable pipeline that extends toward 1,000 categories without rework. The finding that matters most: when an AI benchmark looks bad, check the reference data before blaming the model.
